# FORMA — Creative Refoundation

**Version:** 0.1  
**Status:** Creative direction approved by product owner; implementation in progress; Phase 08 remains unapproved  
**Reference supplied by product owner:** Otsuka / zeroz — https://otsuka-air.jp/  
**Working thesis:** FORMA / A GENERATIVE WORLD OF FORM

---

## 1. Why this reset exists

The current implementation is functional and has a useful technical foundation, but its visual language is still too close to a conventional creative-product landing page: a hero, manifesto, feature cards, and separate tools. Passing tests proves that interactions work; it does not prove that the experience is distinctive.

The product owner supplied seven screenshots from the Otsuka / zeroz experience. They establish a more ambitious reference vocabulary:

- A central dimensional hero object framed by enormous type and a dramatic environmental light source.
- A dark, atmospheric spatial environment with a visible floor/horizon, particles, and strong depth.
- A staged transformation from an object focus into a luminous aperture or whiteout.
- Chapter-like narrative sections with floating, tilted, cropped image planes arranged asymmetrically around central editorial copy.
- A vertical progress/navigation cue that makes a long experience feel like a sequence of connected chapters.
- Strong changes in atmosphere and color between chapters: dark forest, pale mint, deep green, and luminous green.
- A generative-looking particle/energy trail that becomes the dominant composition rather than a decorative background.
- Persistent, restrained navigation that remains legible over changing visual scenes.

These are observations from the supplied screenshots, not claims about the reference site's underlying implementation. We should study the visual behavior, not reproduce its brand, exact layouts, assets, copy, or signature effects.

## 2. New creative thesis

**FORMA is a world that assembles itself around the act of making.**

The experience should feel like entering a living generative instrument. Forms are not static illustrations: they are the protagonists of the story. Typography, spatial objects, light, fields, image planes, and controls belong to one authored system.

The reference's strongest lesson is structural: it does not present a stack of unrelated feature cards. It creates a sequence of visual chapters, each with its own atmosphere and composition, while navigation and progress keep the visitor oriented.

FORMA should build its own sequence around five verbs:

1. **IGNITE** — enter the world; encounter a singular generative form.
2. **DISTURB** — influence it; see the system respond to input.
3. **COMPOSE** — explore distinct visual territories and experiments.
4. **DIRECT** — shape motion, timing, and behavior.
5. **BUILD** — enter the actual creation environment.

This is an original FORMA structure, not a reskin of Otsuka.

## 3. Art direction

### 3.1 Visual world

Retain FORMA's approved identity foundation as the base: Obsidian, Soft Canvas, Electric Lime, Signal Coral, Digital Blue, and Stone Gray; Space Grotesk, Inter, and JetBrains Mono. Do not replace the brand palette with Otsuka's green.

Expand the palette through authored scene atmospheres rather than changing the core identity:
- **Void:** Obsidian, low-contrast structural lines, sparse points of light.
- **Signal:** Electric Lime and Digital Blue used as active energy/material accents.
- **Heat:** Signal Coral as a rare interruption or state marker.
- **Paper:** Soft Canvas as a full-scene contrast chapter, not merely a card background.
- **Afterimage:** layered translucent forms, luminous edges, and controlled bloom-like CSS/WebGL treatment.

Scene color is allowed to shift dramatically, but semantic controls, contrast, and brand recognition must remain consistent.

### 3.2 Composition

- Use a strong underlying grid, then intentionally violate it.
- Allow oversized typography to collide with, frame, or be occluded by spatial objects without hiding essential meaning.
- Use cropped planes, floating panels, off-axis images, deep foreground/background separation, and controlled negative space.
- Make section boundaries feel like scene changes, not card-stack separators.
- Alternate dense, kinetic chapters with quiet editorial pauses.
- Avoid applying one particle effect, one cube, or one gradient to every section.

### 3.3 Signature visual system

Create a FORMA-specific family of generative forms:
- **The Seed:** a stable seed and parameter set that determines a composition.
- **The Core:** one dominant form or cluster that anchors a scene.
- **The Field:** surrounding points, traces, planes, or fragments generated by rules.
- **The Trace:** a visible record of movement, force, or transformation.
- **The Aperture:** a mask/light/shape transition between chapters.
- **The Specimen:** a captured composition presented as an editorial artifact.

Each chapter uses a different combination of these primitives. The system should be recognizable without making every scene look identical.

## 4. New home experience structure

The home page becomes a connected sequence of five immersive chapters, with native scroll and a persistent chapter indicator.

### 00 — PRELUDE / SIGNAL

A short, immediate opening. The FORMA wordmark and a small live signal introduce the world. Avoid a long loading screen or forced intro. The user can enter or skip motion instantly.

### 01 — IGNITE / THE FORM ENGINE

A full-viewport spatial scene. A single original generative sculpture floats in a dark environment with a floor/horizon, restrained particles, light volume, and dimensional typography.

The object is not a decorative CSS cube. It is a parameter-driven composition that can change its topology, arrangement, material, or spatial relationship. Pointer/touch influence changes the scene within bounded limits; a keyboard-accessible Recompose action produces a new seeded arrangement.

Oversized editorial copy frames the object rather than sitting in a conventional left/right hero grid. Side labels communicate the current study and active parameter. A compact scroll cue indicates the next chapter.

### 02 — DISTURB / THE APERTURE

The first scene resolves into an aperture: the central form compresses, opens, or fragments into a luminous field. The transition introduces the FORMA philosophy in a high-contrast quiet zone.

The visitor learns the central premise: form is a system that can be changed. The visual transition must be interruptible and must not delay access to content.

### 03 — COMPOSE / THE CHAPTER FIELD

An editorial sequence of three creative territories:
- **FORM** — geometry, matter, displacement, and generative composition.
- **MOTION** — time, rhythm, easing, trajectory, and kinetic typography.
- **SPACE** — depth, cameras, scenes, and dimensional construction.

Use floating/cropped/tilted image or render planes with varied scale and depth. Chapter text remains the primary semantic anchor. A vertical progress rail indicates chapter position and allows direct navigation. Each territory links to a real destination or is explicitly marked as a future destination; no fake links.

### 04 — DIRECT / THE LIVE STUDY

A full-width generative composition or motion trace responds to real controls. Visitors can alter a small number of meaningful parameters (for example seed, force, density, or speed), see the result immediately, and reset to a known state.

This section previews actual Playground and Motion Lab capabilities rather than simulating a product that does not exist.

### 05 — BUILD / ENTER FORMA

The composition resolves from scattered fragments into a deliberate modular structure. The closing invitation is a spatial threshold into the Playground or, when available, Scene Studio. It should feel like entering the instrument, not clicking a generic CTA card.

## 5. Product surfaces

### Home
A chapter-based immersive narrative with one coherent visual world and a few strong scene changes. It introduces the product and demonstrates real generative interaction.

### Playground
A curatorial exhibition of specimens. Use an irregular spatial index with variable scale, layered previews, and a persistent filter/navigation system. Each experiment opens into its own art-directed stage; do not force every experiment into the same template.

### Motion Lab
A motion instrument, not a settings dashboard. The live stage dominates. Timeline, easing, path, duration, intensity, and playback controls form a compact control rail that can expand contextually. Motion traces should be visible and inspectable.

### Scene Studio
A precise creation environment. The theatrical world should give way to a stable editor with a high-quality viewport, hierarchy, and inspector. Keep the viewport visually rich, but controls and document state must be clear and reliable.

### Archive
A gallery of saved specimens with provenance: generator, version, seed, parameters, and date where available. Visual browsing first; reproducibility and metadata are one step away.

### About
A concise editorial narrative with a few strong visual chapters, not a résumé-like block of cards.

## 6. Motion and transition grammar

Use motion as connective tissue between chapters:
- **Assemble:** fragments converge into a coherent form.
- **Disperse:** a structure separates into controlled elements.
- **Aperture:** a shape or light field reveals the next scene.
- **Parallax:** planes move at distinct depths to establish space.
- **Orbit:** camera/object relationships change with clear spatial intent.
- **Trace:** movement leaves a path that explains force or timing.
- **Morph:** one form changes into another while preserving visual continuity.
- **Cut:** decisive editorial transitions provide rhythm and avoid endless easing.

Native scrolling remains in control. Scroll progress may drive visual state, but no scroll-jacking, trapped sequences, forced waits, or hidden content. Every scene needs an entry state, exit state, and a direct path to navigation.

## 7. Interaction model

Prominent interactions must change the composition or reveal useful information:
- Pointer/touch: bounded influence on the current scene, never required to read or navigate.
- Click/tap: select a chapter, change a parameter, recompose, or enter an experiment.
- Keyboard: navigate chapters and operate all controls without a pointer.
- Scroll: progress through the narrative with a persistent location cue.
- Seed: reproduce the same generative composition where deterministic output is promised.
- Reset: restore the chapter's known default state.

Avoid cursor gimmicks, uncontrolled physics, decorative buttons, or controls with no observable consequence.

## 8. Technical implementation strategy

Do not attempt to make every scene a heavyweight WebGL canvas.

- Use CSS for typography, layout, masks, simple planes, and interface transitions.
- Use Three.js / React Three Fiber for the hero's genuinely spatial generative sculpture and selected experiments where depth, lighting, or geometry warrants it.
- Use deterministic seeded generators for repeatable arrangements.
- Lazy-load each 3D scene behind a client boundary; do not put a global WebGL canvas in the root layout.
- Keep semantic HTML text and navigation outside the canvas.
- Use a static or CSS-rendered fallback when WebGL is unavailable.
- Pause off-screen renderers and dispose geometry/materials/listeners.
- Cap device pixel ratio and geometry/particle budgets; degrade effects on mobile and low-power devices.
- Respect reduced motion by replacing camera movement, continuous particles, and morphing with stable compositions and short state changes.
- Keep the existing Next.js, TypeScript, CSS Modules, design tokens, Supabase, and R3F architecture decisions intact.

The reference screenshots do not establish the original site's engine, so no assumptions about its stack are carried into FORMA.

## 9. Accessibility, performance, and quality bar

- No essential text inside a canvas.
- Persistent, semantic navigation and chapter progress.
- Visible focus, keyboard-operable controls, touch equivalents, and reduced-motion alternatives.
- Avoid strobing, excessive bloom, unreadable text over bright scenes, and motion that obscures content.
- Keep a static first paint; do not require a long preloader.
- Avoid simultaneous heavy canvases.
- Test at 320, 375, 768, 1024, and 1440px widths.
- Test WebGL unavailable, reduced motion, keyboard-only, touch, and low-power behavior.
- Capture screenshots/video of each chapter for visual review; passing unit/E2E tests alone is not visual approval.

## 13. Orbit-centered identity system — approved 2026-11-23

The product owner added a defining Home composition: **FORMA sits at the gravitational center**, with meaningful forms orbiting around it. This is the signature identity system, not an ornamental logo lockup.

### The central core

The FORMA wordmark sits in a stable, legible central core. It may be rendered as a dimensional typographic/graphic object in the 3D scene, but the accessible semantic brand name remains real HTML. The center should feel anchored while the environment around it is alive.

### The orbiting specimens

Use a deliberately curated set of orbiting objects, each with a clear relationship to a product capability:

- **FORM / Geometry specimen:** a modular, faceted, parametric object that can assemble, split, and change topology.
- **MOTION / Trace specimen:** a ribbon, trajectory, or kinetic marker that communicates time, direction, and rhythm.
- **TYPE / Glyph specimen:** a typographic fragment or letterform treated as a spatial object, not an arbitrary floating word.
- **SPACE / Frame specimen:** a wireframe volume, portal, or camera-framing structure that communicates depth and scene construction.
- **FIELD / Seed specimen:** a bounded cluster of points/fragments whose arrangement is determined by a visible seed/rule.

### Orbital behavior

- Specimens travel on different planes, eccentricities, directions, and speeds; avoid a perfectly even solar-system diagram.
- The orbital paths should intersect visually without objects clipping through the central identity in confusing ways.
- Pointer/touch influence can tilt the viewpoint or gently perturb orbital paths within bounded limits.
- Selecting a specimen brings it into focus, changes its behavior/material, reveals its name and short premise, and provides a real route to the relevant feature.
- A seeded “recompose” action changes a set of orbital parameters deterministically; show the seed or provide a reproducible state.
- Scroll progress evolves the composition across the chapter narrative: converge, disturb, open the aperture, disperse into territories, then assemble toward creation.
- Motion is purposeful and interruptible. The user can navigate away at any time.

### Visual treatment

The orbital scene should combine an atmospheric environment, a ground/horizon or spatial reference plane, dimensional lighting, restrained particle detail, large editorial typography, and a few strong color/material contrasts. Bloom and glow are controlled effects, not a permanent wash over the screen. The orbiting forms must each have unique silhouettes and materials; no repetitive collection of generic spheres.

### Mobile and accessibility behavior

On narrow screens, transform the orbit into a more compact, touch-selectable constellation with fewer simultaneously active objects and simplified paths. A semantic list of the same territories remains available. Reduced motion freezes the orbit into an authored composition and replaces continuous movement with explicit selection/recompose transitions. Keyboard users can move between specimens and select each one without a pointer.

## 14. Motion and 3D library policy

### Approved orchestration

- **GSAP:** primary timeline and choreography system for DOM/3D state coordination, scene entrance/exit, staged reveals, and selected scroll-linked sequences.
- **@gsap/react:** React lifecycle integration for GSAP timelines and scoped cleanup.
- **ScrollTrigger:** use for progressive chapter choreography; native scrolling remains in control.
- **Flip:** consider only for meaningful layout/state transitions where FLIP is better than a simple CSS transition; do not add a visual trick merely to use a plugin.

### 3D responsibilities

- **Three.js + React Three Fiber:** core 3D scene, camera, materials, meshes, custom geometry, and render lifecycle.
- **Drei:** selected utilities for controls, loaders, helpers, and common scene primitives; avoid pulling broad helpers into unrelated routes.
- **@react-three/postprocessing:** candidate for carefully budgeted visual treatments (for example, selective bloom) after measuring the visual benefit and mobile GPU cost.
- **Rapier:** candidate only for experiences that truly need collisions or rigid-body simulation. The orbit itself should be deterministic and parameter-driven, not a physics toy.
- **Theatre.js:** defer unless a concrete scene-authoring need exceeds GSAP's timeline workflow.

### Avoid overlapping stacks

Do not add Framer Motion, Motion One, Lenis, or a second physics/animation engine simply to increase library count. CSS handles small interface transitions. GSAP handles orchestration. R3F/Three handles spatial rendering. Native scroll remains the source of scroll position.

No third-party library is permitted to replace accessibility semantics, reduced-motion behavior, fallbacks, or resource budgets.

### Installation gate

The Phase 08 branch now includes GSAP 3.15.1, @gsap/react 2.1.2, Three.js 0.187.0, React Three Fiber 9.8.2, and Drei 10.7.9. These exact versions are pinned in package.json and package-lock.json. A clean Node 22 npm ci and npm ls dependency resolution succeeded. Do not add further libraries without a concrete feature, compatibility check, and runtime/performance justification.
