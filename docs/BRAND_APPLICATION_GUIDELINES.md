# FORMA — Brand Application Guidelines

**Phase:** 04 — Visual Design System  
**Status:** Proposed application system; pending product-owner approval  
**Identity direction:** Modular Frame

This document translates the proposed identity into repeatable rules for product surfaces, editorial material, and future launch assets. It does not declare the logo final.

## 1. Identity hierarchy

1. **Primary signature:** horizontal symbol + FORMA wordmark. Use in the site header, presentation covers, and formal brand introductions.
2. **Secondary signature:** standalone Modular Frame mark. Use where the name is already visible or space is constrained.
3. **Micro signature:** simplified favicon/app mark. Use only at compact sizes; never substitute it for the full signature in a first brand introduction.

The symbol and wordmark are a single lockup. Do not independently resize, recolor, or rearrange their parts.

## 2. Clear space and sizing

Use the width of the symbol's vertical spine as the minimum clear-space unit (**x**). Keep at least x clear on every side of the symbol and lockup. Increase this space whenever the surrounding layout allows.

Provisional digital minimums, pending rendered-size review:
- Horizontal lockup: 120 px wide.
- Standalone mark: 16 px, using the simplified favicon geometry at 16 px.
- Preferred standalone mark: 24 px or larger.
- Editorial / hero lockup: size to the composition; avoid making it compete with the main message.

Do not use the full lockup when the wordmark becomes difficult to read. These are working minimums, not final optical certification.

## 3. Color applications

- Default light surface: Obsidian mark on Soft Canvas.
- Default dark surface: Soft Canvas mark on Obsidian.
- Single-color reproduction: solid black or white.
- Electric Lime: supporting highlight, active state, or occasional brand field; not a required logo color.
- Signal Coral and Digital Blue: expressive interface accents, not alternate logo colors.

Never place the mark on a busy image or low-contrast surface. Do not add gradients, glow, bevels, shadows, strokes, or independent accent colors to the logo.

## 4. Supporting graphic language

The identity's visual vocabulary is structural and transformational:
- **Frame:** open corners, aligned edges, measured crops, and clear negative space.
- **Shift:** repeated planes or outlines with a small, consistent offset to express a state change.
- **Modular rhythm:** repeated rectangular units, controlled spacing, and deliberate alignment.
- **Contrast:** large quiet fields with a single purposeful accent.

Use these devices to explain a transition, organize content, or establish a composition. Do not apply them as wallpaper, random decoration, or a substitute for content hierarchy.

Reference SVGs:
- `graphic-frame.svg` — open structural frame.
- `graphic-form-shift.svg` — three-state offset transformation.

## 5. Typography in application

- Space Grotesk: headlines, display statements, and brand wordmark reference.
- Inter: body copy, navigation, controls, and reading text.
- JetBrains Mono: coordinates, labels, parameters, metadata, and technical annotations.

Use hierarchy and spacing before relying on weight or color. Avoid long all-caps passages. Mono is an accent for information architecture, not the default body face.

## 6. Composition rules

- Establish a clear alignment edge and repeat it.
- Let negative space do real work.
- Prefer one expressive device per composition.
- Use asymmetry when it improves hierarchy, not as an automatic style.
- Keep primary actions visually obvious and secondary actions quiet.
- Do not sacrifice readability to preserve a dramatic layout.

## 7. Product touchpoints

| Touchpoint | Recommended treatment |
|---|---|
| Global navigation | Compact standalone mark + readable wordmark or full lockup |
| Home hero | Large editorial typography; one structural or shift motif |
| Playground | Variable editorial preview compositions; restrained labels |
| Motion Lab | Shift motif used to clarify motion/state changes |
| Scene Studio | Mark stays quiet; controls and canvas own the visual hierarchy |
| Archive / case studies | Strong typographic hierarchy, metadata in mono, generous whitespace |
| Favicon | Simplified mark only |
| Social / presentation cover | Full lockup with clear space and a single supporting graphic device |

## 8. Motion

Motion should communicate construction, transition, or feedback. If the mark animates, use a one-time, short transition from frame to F to settled mark. Never loop it in navigation. Honor `prefers-reduced-motion` by showing the final static state immediately.

## 9. Do not

- Do not rotate, stretch, skew, outline, or add 3D treatment to the mark.
- Do not combine all three logo explorations literally into one crowded emblem.
- Do not place a permanent decorative frame around every section.
- Do not use accent colors as a substitute for accessible contrast.
- Do not invent new logo variants for individual pages.
- Do not treat the proposed assets as trademark-cleared.

## 10. Review before approval

- Inspect the mark at 16 / 24 / 32 / 48 px on light and dark backgrounds.
- Check optical alignment in the lockup, not only geometric alignment.
- Test the favicon in a real browser tab.
- Review contrast and clarity in rendered product contexts.
- Compare the mark against relevant products and registered marks before commercial use.

**Approval note:** This is a proposed application guide. Final logo approval remains with the product owner.
