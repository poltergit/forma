# FORMA — Force Grammar

**Version:** 1.0  
**Principle:** Every form is evidence of a force.

This document defines what FORMA's generative forms mean and how that meaning must survive across visuals, motion, interaction, and interface language.

## The four primitives

| Primitive | Meaning | What it must reveal |
|---|---|---|
| Matter | The subject being shaped | A recognizable specimen with material, boundary, and volume |
| Force | The condition acting on matter | A cause: attraction, pressure, rhythm, constraint, or gravity |
| Trace | Evidence of change | Previous positions, displaced contours, paths, echoes, or imprints |
| Notation | Human interpretation | Labels, coordinates, measurements, and short explanations |

A composition should make at least one relationship between these primitives visible. An isolated abstract object is not enough.

## Three foundational force studies

### 01 — Orbit / Attraction

**Meaning:** Ideas do not exist alone. They are shaped by relationships, proximity, gravity, and mutual influence.

**Visual behavior:**
- Objects travel along curved paths around a shared center.
- Their spacing changes as attraction strengthens or weakens.
- Elliptical traces reveal the path already traveled.
- The central specimen subtly twists with the field.

**Interaction contract:** Pointer movement changes the field's center. The visitor sees the system reorganize around a new point of influence.

**Use when:** Showing relationships, networks, dependencies, collaboration, or an idea gathering context.

### 02 — Fracture / Pressure

**Meaning:** Pressure exposes hidden structure. Breaking a form is not destruction for spectacle; it is a way to inspect its internal logic.

**Visual behavior:**
- A continuous surface develops stress lines and sharp discontinuities.
- Fragments move outward from the point of greatest tension.
- Wireframe shards expose the geometry beneath the surface.
- The Afterform records the shape before it separated.

**Interaction contract:** Pointer movement disturbs the field; fragments and stress contours respond without becoming arbitrary particles.

**Use when:** Showing critique, experimentation, disruption, decomposition, or the questioning of assumptions.

### 03 — Resolve / Alignment

**Meaning:** A new structure emerges when separate elements find a shared order. Resolution is not a return to the original state; it is a changed arrangement.

**Visual behavior:**
- Fragments move toward a common structure.
- Contours become concentric and legible.
- The Afterform holds an earlier silhouette while the new form settles.
- Motion decelerates into a deliberate state of rest.

**Interaction contract:** Changes to intensity, rhythm, and fragment count alter how quickly and tightly the specimen converges.

**Use when:** Showing synthesis, composition, refinement, systems coming together, or an idea becoming tangible.

## Motion vocabulary

| Verb | Physical interpretation | Narrative role |
|---|---|---|
| Pull | Attraction toward a field or center | Relationship |
| Push | Displacement away from a force | Intervention |
| Bend | Deformation under constraint | Adaptation |
| Break | Separation under pressure | Discovery |
| Repeat | Iteration with controlled offset | Rhythm / learning |
| Resolve | Alignment and settling | Emergence |

Every animation must answer three questions:
1. What caused the movement?
2. What changed because of it?
3. What trace or information remains afterward?

If those questions have no clear answers, the animation is decoration and should be removed.

## Material and color semantics

- **Hyperlime:** a decisive event, active force, or moment of transformation.
- **Signal Flame:** pressure, interruption, friction, and impact.
- **Ultraviolet:** spatial depth, invisible fields, and dimensional relationships.
- **Laser Cyan:** continuity, energy transfer, and paths through space.
- **Porcelain:** the readable surface, quiet contrast, and human interpretation.
- **Abyss:** depth, unknown space, and the environment around a specimen.

Do not use all signals at once by default. Color should clarify the force or state, not merely increase visual intensity.

## Cross-feature application

- **Landing / Manifestation:** introduce the four primitives and let visitors change the force acting on one specimen.
- **Playground / Open Field:** let visitors compose matter, choose a generator, and inspect the consequences of changing parameters.
- **Motion Lab / Force Room:** expose the relationship between input, motion path, timing, and trace.
- **Scene Studio / Workbench:** preserve the object as the primary subject; controls should explain how light, camera, and material alter it.
- **Archive / Specimen Index:** preserve the evidence of a past experiment: its seed, parameters, traces, and the decisions that produced it.

## Acceptance criteria for future generative work

- A form has a named role in the visual story.
- Its motion has a physical or conceptual cause.
- At least one input has a legible effect on the form.
- The resulting state is understandable without relying on color alone.
- A trace, annotation, or state label helps explain what changed.
- The composition has moments of stillness.
- Reduced-motion users receive a meaningful static representation.
- The experience remains usable on touch devices and at constrained GPU budgets.

**FORMA rule: Do not add a shape until you can explain what it means. Do not animate it until you can explain why it moves.**
