# FORMA — Creative DNA

**Version:** 1.1  
**Phase:** Neon Relic — Visual Identity  
**Status:** Working foundation for owner review  
**Creative premise:** A world in which matter is always becoming.

FORMA is an independent digital atelier: a place to shape ideas by changing their conditions. Its identity is not a skin placed over a tool. The interface, the objects, the motion, and the traces left by interaction are all part of the same creative system.

The ambition is not to look like another experimental studio website. FORMA must build a recognizable visual grammar that can only belong to FORMA.

## 1. The central idea — Matter in Motion

**Nothing is finished. Everything is becoming.**

A form is never treated as a static object. It is the temporary result of forces: pressure, rhythm, gravity, repetition, collision, distortion, and time.

This gives FORMA a creative subject of its own: not "abstract 3D" in general, but the visible behavior of matter as it changes state.

The identity should make those forces legible. It should show a form, the conditions acting on it, and the trace of what just happened.

### The four identity primitives

1. **Matter** — the specimen: a shape, letterform, surface, scene, or composition being explored.
2. **Force** — the condition: a field, vector, constraint, rhythm, collision, or parameter changing the specimen.
3. **Trace** — the evidence: a contour echo, displaced duplicate, path, registration mark, or imprint showing the history of change.
4. **Notation** — the human intelligence: labels, coordinates, measurements, annotations, indices, and short editorial statements that make the experiment understandable.

These four primitives are the core visual vocabulary. A screen does not need all four at once, but every major experience should be built from at least one and preferably a meaningful relationship between two or more.

## 2. Signature visual device — The Afterform

FORMA's recognizable signature is **the Afterform**: a visible trace of a shape's previous or possible state.

Examples:
- A wireframe contour offset behind a solid object.
- A second typographic impression slightly displaced from the primary letterform.
- A motion path that remains briefly after an object moves.
- A registration cross marking where a shape used to be.
- A stack of slightly different silhouettes showing a transformation.
- A flat outline that anticipates a 3D object before it resolves.

The Afterform is not a generic glow, particle trail, or decorative duplicate. It must communicate transformation, direction, repetition, or memory.

Use it as a recurring brand signature across the landing experience, studies, Motion Lab, and Scene Studio. Vary its medium and scale rather than repeating one effect everywhere.

## 3. Visual personality

FORMA is:
- **Restless:** compositions feel in progress, not frozen.
- **Art-directed:** every disruption has a reason.
- **Tactile:** surfaces and materials have character.
- **Intelligent:** technical notation makes experimentation legible.
- **Playful:** surprise is welcome; confusion is not.
- **Precise beneath the mess:** grids, alignment, spacing, and behavior remain deliberate.

FORMA is not:
- A generic SaaS dashboard with a 3D hero.
- A sterile design system showcase.
- A maximalist collage on every screen.
- A random collection of gradients, blobs, glass panels, and floating objects.
- A clone of another studio's layouts, motion, artwork, or brand assets.

**Creative rule:** composition may be irregular; interaction must remain understandable.

## 4. Color — Neon Relic / Voltage / Afterhours

Color is used as material and information, not as a universal decoration layer. The identity is now nocturnal and high-contrast: one abyssal ground, one porcelain counter-ground, and four electric signals.

| Token | Name | HEX | Purpose |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Porcelain | Counter-ground | `#F4F0E8` | Editorial contrast, typography, breathing room |
| Abyss | Primary ground | `#100D18` | Immersive environments, navigation, deep contrast |
| Hyperlime | Disruption | `#D9FF3F` | Active state, generative energy, sharp punctuation |
| Signal Flame | Impact | `#FF5A3C` | Human warmth, interruption, high-energy emphasis |
| Ultraviolet | Spatial signal | `#875BFF` | Depth, dimensionality, selected technical state |
| Laser Cyan | Organic counterpoint | `#54F2D2` | Luminous material, energy, and spatial trails |

### Color behavior
- Porcelain and Abyss establish the stage. Do not place every section on the same ground.
- Hyperlime is a punctuation mark. It should be rare enough to stay powerful.
- Signal Flame is the human signal: use it for moments that need warmth or impact.
- Ultraviolet belongs to spatial and dimensional systems, not every link and button.
- Laser Cyan is a balancing material, not a mandatory accent.
- Avoid using all accents at equal strength in one composition.
- Text contrast always takes priority over color expression.

### Suggested color ratios, not rigid quotas
A typical editorial surface may use 70–85% ground, 10–20% supporting structure, and 3–8% high-energy signal. Immersive experiments can break this ratio when the composition has a clear focal hierarchy.

## 5. Typography — Voice / Instruction / Measurement

Keep the existing type family roles:
- **Space Grotesk — Voice:** large editorial statements, expressive display, distinctive line breaks.
- **Inter — Instruction:** interface, reading, labels that need sustained clarity.
- **JetBrains Mono — Measurement:** coordinates, indices, parameter values, status, and technical notation.

### Typography behavior
- Use oversized display type as a compositional object, not merely a heading style.
- Crop, overlap, rotate, or displace type only when the reading order remains clear.
- Use short labels and numbered annotations to give abstract compositions context.
- Technical labels should be small but readable; never use tiny type as fake sophistication.
- Avoid applying uppercase monospace to every piece of interface copy.

## 6. Composition grammar

FORMA uses a **loose grid with hard anchors**.

Hard anchors:
- A clear primary subject.
- A consistent alignment edge or registration line.
- An understandable reading or interaction path.
- Stable placement for essential navigation and controls.

Creative freedoms:
- Offset columns and baselines.
- Oversized typography that crosses grid boundaries.
- Cropped specimens and partial forms.
- Layered planes and asymmetrical negative space.
- Labels that sit outside the object they describe.
- Intentional overlap between image, type, and notation.
- Different section structures within one page.

Do not force every page into a card grid. Use cards only when the content is genuinely a collection of comparable items. Prefer open compositions, split fields, specimen sheets, workbenches, rails, and editorial sequences.

## 7. Shape and material grammar

The form vocabulary should feel authored, not selected from a library of generic blobs.

### Shape families
- **Constructed:** frames, planes, grids, lattices, extrusions, folded surfaces.
- **Organic:** softened masses, stretched membranes, fluid transitions, irregular contours.
- **Kinetic:** repeated silhouettes, ribbons, arcs, trajectories, oscillations.
- **Typographic:** extruded letters, sliced glyphs, variable scale, typographic specimens.

### Material contrast
Pair contrasting behaviors: matte against polished, opaque against translucent, rigid against flexible, flat against volumetric. Do not make everything glass, chrome, or iridescent. A single unusual material is stronger when surrounded by restraint.

### Generative integrity
- Generative output should be seeded and reproducible where users need to revisit or export a study.
- Parameters should visibly affect the output.
- Randomness should be bounded by a compositional rule.
- A generated result should retain enough structure to feel authored rather than arbitrary.
- Avoid gratuitous perpetual movement; give the work moments of stillness.

## 8. Motion — Force, displacement, echo, settle

Motion is the visible behavior of matter.

Core motion verbs:
- **Pull** — attraction, orbit, magnetic alignment.
- **Push** — displacement, pressure, recoil.
- **Bend** — deformation, easing, elastic response.
- **Repeat** — rhythm, iteration, offset copies.
- **Break** — fragmentation, separation, controlled scatter.
- **Resolve** — assembly, alignment, settling into a final state.

Motion should have weight, timing, and a visible cause. Prefer a few memorable transitions over constant ambient movement.

### Motion rules
- Interaction feedback should be immediate.
- Large transitions may have anticipation and settle, but never delay access to content.
- Scroll motion should support narrative and not hijack the page.
- The Afterform may persist as a short-lived trace or as a deliberate static imprint.
- Respect reduced-motion preferences with meaningful static alternatives.
- Avoid excessive parallax, cursor-chasing gimmicks, and motion that harms performance or legibility.

## 9. Spatial identity

3D is a native creative medium, not a requirement for every screen.

Use spatial depth when it helps explain:
- Relationships between forms.
- Material and light.
- Transformation over time.
- Composition and camera perspective.
- The relationship between object, field, and environment.

The camera is part of the art direction. Establish intentional viewpoints and focal distance. Keep controls accessible and provide sensible quality fallbacks. Do not let a full-screen 3D scene obscure the product's purpose.

## 10. Meaning before visualization

Every visible form must communicate a relationship between matter, force, trace, and intention. Before adding a new object or animation, define what it represents, what causes it to move, what changes through interaction, and what evidence remains afterward. FORMA's foundational force studies are Orbit (attraction and relationships), Fracture (pressure and exposed structure), and Resolve (alignment and emergence). Their visual behaviors and acceptance criteria are specified in [FORMA Force Grammar](./FORMA_FORCE_GRAMMAR.md). An effect without a clear cause, consequence, or trace is decoration and does not belong in the atelier.

## 11. Feature personalities — one atelier, five rooms

Each experience has its own visual language, composition, rhythm, and interaction logic. They share the same identity primitives and color semantics, but must never feel like the same template with different headings. See [FORMA Room Personalities](./FORMA_ROOM_PERSONALITIES.md) for the complete art-direction and interaction contracts.

| Experience | Personality | Dominant primitives | Composition |
|---|---|---|---|
| Landing | The Manifestation | Matter + Trace + Notation | A signature specimen, oversized voice, orbiting annotations, a sense of emergence |
| Playground | The Open Field | Matter + Force | Loose, surprising, exploratory; studies feel like experiments pinned to a wall |
| Scene Studio | The Workbench | Matter + Notation | Compact, viewport-first, dense but calm; tools support the specimen rather than compete with it |
| Motion Lab | The Force Room | Force + Trace | Time, paths, echoes, and rhythm are visible; controls behave like instruments |
| Archive | The Specimen Index | Trace + Notation | Curated, searchable records of work and its variations |

A shared identity does not mean identical layouts. Feature personality is expressed through composition, density, color balance, and interaction—not by changing the logo or inventing a new unrelated palette per page.

## 12. Interface behavior and usability

- Essential navigation and core actions remain predictable.
- Dense creative controls should be grouped by task and use progressive disclosure.
- Workspaces should fit the available viewport where appropriate; secondary panels scroll internally instead of creating unnecessary page length.
- The canvas or primary work area receives the strongest visual priority.
- Empty, loading, saving, error, and success states must be designed, not left as default browser behavior.
- Responsive layouts should re-compose the experience, not merely shrink desktop columns.
- Keyboard access, focus visibility, contrast, and reduced motion are part of the identity.

## 13. Sound, texture, and environmental detail

Sound is optional and off by default. If introduced, it must be user-initiated, controllable, and useful.

Texture may include paper grain, registration marks, fine rules, halftone, subtle noise, or measured grid systems. Texture should be quiet enough not to compete with text or reduce contrast. Avoid adding noise overlays to every surface.

## 14. Anti-patterns

Reject:
- Generic startup gradients as the primary identity.
- Repetitive rounded cards as the default layout.
- Glassmorphism without a functional reason.
- Random floating objects with no relationship to the content.
- Fake controls, fake metrics, fake testimonials, or fake functionality.
- Constant motion that adds no information.
- Overuse of neon, glow, blur, and chrome.
- Decorative microtype that is unreadable.
- A different unrelated design language for every feature.
- Copying another studio's distinctive composition, animation, code, imagery, or identity.

## 15. Acceptance checklist

A major visual decision should pass these questions:

1. Does it express Matter, Force, Trace, or Notation?
2. Is there a clear focal point and reading path?
3. Does the visual behavior have meaning or cause?
4. Is the composition distinctive without relying on generic effects?
5. Does the interaction remain understandable and accessible?
6. Does it work responsively and with reduced motion?
7. Does it preserve performance and the user's work?
8. Would this still feel like FORMA without the wordmark?

If the answer to the first, second, or fifth question is no, rethink the design.
